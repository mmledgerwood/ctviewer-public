What is this?

Dicom Viewer is a lightweight, local tool for browsing and visualizing DICOM CT studies straight from a folder on your computer — no upload, no server beyond your own machine. It's built on NiiVue, a WebGL medical-imaging viewer, with a custom interface layered on top for navigating multiple studies, adjusting the image, measuring distances, and viewing the volume in 2D, 3D, and freely-angled ("oblique") slices.

How to Use

Opening a study
Click Open Folder… in the sidebar and choose a folder containing one or more DICOM studies (or point it straight at a single study or series). Studies then appear in the sidebar list, automatically grouped if their folder names follow a GROUP^DAY convention (e.g. MVSG^7_... groups under "MVSG" with day 7).
Loading a study
Click a study in the list to load it. The first click loads it at whatever slice thickness setting is currently selected (native resolution by default).
Slice thickness
Leave this field blank to load the full native resolution — every slice, untouched. Set a thickness in mm larger than the scan's native slice spacing to load a smaller, faster volume instead: the server reads every native slice and averages groups of them together into fewer, thicker output slices. This is higher quality (every bit of data contributes) but doesn't save any read time, since every file still has to be read.
Fast preview
Only takes effect when a slice thickness is also set. Instead of reading every slice and averaging, it probes the native spacing, works out how many slices to skip to roughly hit your target thickness, and then only reads those — the skipped files are never downloaded or opened at all. This is the fastest option, especially when files are slow cloud downloads (e.g. Google Drive), at the cost of a bit of detail (skipped slices decimate rather than average).
After changing slice thickness or fast preview, click the already-selected study again to re-load it with the new setting.

Toolbar

The row of tools across the top of the window.

Interaction modes

Crosshair default mode

Click in any 2D panel (Axial/Sagittal/Coronal) to move the crosshair there — it stays in sync across every view, including the 3D render, any pop-out window, and the Plane Viewer. In the 3D panel, a click picks a point on the volume's surface; dragging orbits the view.

Pan / Zoom

Drag to move the image within a panel; scroll to zoom in or out (replaces stepping through 2D slices while this mode is active). Right-click-drag pans in any mode. In the 3D panel, dragging moves the camera sideways instead of orbiting.

Measure

Switches the right-hand sidebar to the Measure panel and switches dragging in a 2D panel from moving the crosshair/panning to drawing whichever measurement tool is selected there. Opening Measure closes Image Adjustments if it was open (they share the same sidebar slot); switching back to Crosshair, Pan/Zoom, or Rotate 3D closes the Measure panel again. See Measure below for the full tool set and table/export options.

Rotate 3D

Drag anywhere in the 3D panel to orbit it — it feels like grabbing the object itself and turning it. Rotation only happens while the mouse button is held, and stops the instant you release it.

Other toolbar buttons

Adjust closed by default

Opens the Image Adjustments panel in the right-hand sidebar — Brightness, Contrast, Histogram, and the 3D Render clip slab. Closed by default so the sidebar stays out of the way until you need it; opening Measure closes it, and vice versa.

Undo / Redo

Undo reverses the last discrete action — brightness/contrast, clip slab, view switch, pan/zoom/rotate, and so on. Redo re-applies an undone action; taking any new action clears the redo history.

Reset view

Resets pan, zoom, 3D rotation, brightness, contrast, and the clip slab back to their defaults, and returns to the 4-Panel layout.

Info

Opens this documentation page in its own window.

Views

Click the grid icon in the toolbar to open the Views menu — a grid of layout cards for switching the main viewport.

Axial / Sagittal / Coronal / 3D Rendering 1 2 3 4

Click the card to switch the main viewport to that single view. Click the small expand icon in the card's corner instead to open a larger version of that same view in a pop-out window, without changing the main viewport's layout.

4-Panel 5

The default layout — Axial, Sagittal, Coronal, and the 3D render shown together. Each 2D panel's border and crosshair lines are color-coded to match the plane they represent (axial = red, coronal = green, sagittal = blue), and those same colors appear as thin lines through the crosshair in the 3D render too.

The freely-angled Plane Viewer has its own dedicated toolbar button (next to this Views menu) rather than a card here, since it isn't one of the volume's standard axis-aligned planes — see Plane Viewer below.

Orientation letters

The single letters along the edges of each 2D panel (Axial/Sagittal/Coronal) are standard radiological orientation markers — they say which anatomical direction that edge of the image faces, not a label for the panel itself:

S = Superior (toward the head)  ·  I = Inferior (toward the feet)  ·  A = Anterior (front)  ·  P = Posterior (back)  ·  L = Left  ·  R = Right

Which pair shows up depends on the plane: Axial shows A/P and L/R (it's a cut perpendicular to the body's long axis, so Superior/Inferior don't apply); Sagittal shows S/I and A/P; Coronal shows S/I and L/R.

Side Panels

The right-hand sidebar holds two mutually-exclusive panels — Image Adjustments and Measure — opened from their matching toolbar buttons, plus the always-visible Series Info section at the top of the left sidebar.

Image Adjustments

Brightness

Shifts the whole image lighter or darker (moves the window level) without changing contrast.

Contrast

Steepens or flattens the light-to-dark transition (changes the window width) without shifting the midpoint.

Histogram

Shows the distribution of intensities in the loaded volume. The two labels under it give the minimum and maximum values in Hounsfield Units (HU).

3D Render — Clip Slab

A dual-handle slider: drag the left handle to slice away the bottom of the 3D render, and the right handle to slice away the top. Each side moves independently, letting you carve out just the slab of the volume you want to see in 3D.

Measure

Opening the Measure panel shows a grid of tools — Annotation, Line, Rectangle, Ellipse, and Circle — plus Undo last and Clear all buttons. Pick a tool, then drag in an Axial, Sagittal, or Coronal panel to draw it. Any number of measurements, of any mix of tools, can be on screen at once, and each stays attached to the exact slice it was drawn on — step to a different slice and it disappears; come back and it reappears.

Measuring from the Plane Viewer pop-out

While Measure is active in the main window, the same tool also works inside an open Plane Viewer pop-out window — drag in its plane-slice panel to draw on that freely-angled plane. All of the controls (tool choice, table, Active Measurement box, CSV export) stay in the main window's sidebar; the pop-out just mirrors the current tool and lets you draw with the mouse there. A shape drawn on an oblique plane stays tied to that exact tilt/rotation/depth, the same way a main-panel shape stays tied to its slice.

Annotation

Drag to place an arrow, then type a label for it when prompted. The label sits at the tail end of the arrow (where you started dragging), not at the arrowhead.

Line, Rectangle, Ellipse, Circle

Drag to draw. Line reports its length in mm; Rectangle, Ellipse, and Circle report their area in mm². Circle is a true circle — it constrains Ellipse's drag to equal width and height.

Active Measurement box

While you're dragging, this box live-updates with the cursor's mm position and a histogram of the pixels under the in-progress shape. Once you release, it switches to showing whichever measurement is selected — click any shape to select it (or right-click it, see below) — and stays visible until you select something else, delete it, or clear all. Two checkboxes here, Include histogram values and Export histograms, control what gets carried into the table when you click Move to table (see below); Delete removes the currently-selected measurement from the image and the table together.

Right-click menu (main panels)

Right-clicking directly on a shape in an Axial/Sagittal/Coronal panel opens a small menu with Delete measurement and Add to table — a quicker path to the table than selecting it and clicking Move to table (though without the histogram checkboxes, which only live in the Active Measurement box).

Table

Each row added via Move to table or the right-click menu gets a number, an editable free-text label (click into the cell and type), and its length/area value. Rows that had Include histogram values checked also show summary stats (mean/stdev/min/max) under the value. The × button removes just that table row; deleting the underlying shape itself (via the Active Measurement box or the right-click menu) removes its table row too.

Export CSV

Downloads the table as-is — row number, label, value, and the histogram summary stats (if present) — as a .csv file.

Export Histograms

Downloads a separate .csv with the full per-bin histogram for every table row that had Export histograms checked when it was added — one block per measurement, each labeled with its row number and text, followed by a row of voxel counts and a row of the corresponding HU bin values underneath.

Cursor & Series Info

Cursor shows the mm position, voxel index, and HU (Hounsfield Unit) value under the crosshair, live. Series Info, at the top of the left sidebar above Open Folder, shows the loaded study's name, series description, slice count, and voxel size.

Pop-out Window

A larger, dedicated view of a single plane — opened from a Views card's expand icon. (The Plane Viewer below always opens this way too, straight into its own separate window.)

The crosshair stays in sync with the main window in both directions: move it in the pop-out or the main view and the other follows immediately. For the "3D Rendering" pop-out, dragging rotates both the pop-out and the main 3D view together, and vice versa.

Pop out to a separate window

The small icon next to the pop-out's ✕ close button moves the view into a genuine, separate browser window — one you can drag anywhere on screen, including outside the main browser window entirely (e.g. onto a second monitor). Close that window like any other to bring the view back.

Plane Viewer

A freely-angled slice through the volume, independent of the standard Axial/Sagittal/Coronal planes — opened from its own Plane Viewer button in the toolbar, which lets you pick a starting view (Axial, Sagittal, or Coronal) to tilt from. It always opens in its own separate browser window, with two panels: the plane slice on the left and a 3D View on the right, open automatically from the start.

The Plane Controls header sits above the three sliders, each element lined up over the slider it relates to: the label, the circular-arrow Reset button, and the Crosshair/Pan/Zoom mode buttons over Tilt; the Plane/Object lock switch over Rotate; and the 3D View switch over Depth. Reset restores Tilt, Rotate, Depth, zoom, pan, the crosshair, and the 3D View panel's camera/position to how they were the moment this window opened — not wherever the main window has moved on to since. It leaves the mode buttons and the lock switch alone, since those are tool choices rather than positions.

Tilt, Rotate, and Depth are all centered at their own midpoint — a tick mark there marks the plane's original position on each. Tilt and Rotate are offsets from the starting angle, not absolute angles: dragging Tilt left dips the right side of the plane down (right dips the left side down), and dragging Rotate left or right turns the plane that way — both pivoting around the crosshair, which stays fixed in place. Depth still moves the plane along its own normal, away from the crosshair.

The window has its own Crosshair and Pan/Zoom mode buttons (independent of the main window's toolbar) — and they apply separately to each panel, so the plane slice and the 3D View can be framed differently. With Crosshair active — the default — a click moves the crosshair to that point (synced to every other view, including the main window), while dragging tilts/rotates the plane slice (or orbits the 3D View's camera), and scrolling moves the plane through the volume (depth). With Pan/Zoom active, dragging pans whichever panel you drag in and scrolling zooms it, independently of the other panel.

A small yellow crosshair marks your current position within the plane slice, and a translucent purple plane appears in the main window's 3D rendering showing exactly where that slice is being cut from. The crosshair always sits at the plane's center: moving Depth carries it along with the plane, and clicking a new point resets Depth so the plane recenters there.

The 3D View switch shows/hides a second panel with the same plane indicator in its own 3D view, tracking the main window's 3D rotation both ways — open automatically whenever this window loads. Clicking a point on the volume there also moves the crosshair — same as clicking the plane slice — while dragging orbits that panel's camera (in Crosshair mode; Pan/Zoom mode pans instead, moving the plane and the volume together as one).

The Plane/Object switch picks what the Tilt/Rotate/Depth sliders actually move in the 3D View panel. Plane (default) is a plain slider: the plane moves through a fixed volume, same as the plane slice panel always shows. Object instead holds the plane visually fixed and moves the volume to match — Tilt/Rotate orbit that panel's camera around it, and Depth slides the volume up or down past the fixed plane. Either way, the plane slice panel and the main window's plane indicator always show the same cut; the switch only changes which one appears to move in the 3D View panel.
